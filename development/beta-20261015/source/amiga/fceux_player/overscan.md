# Overscan, borders and a 4:3 picture

ZZNes Library keeps the complete NES frame's **display aspect** at 4:3. Select
the shape of your monitor, and configure the monitor to fill its panel with
the 320×240 signal. A 4:3 monitor uses a 320×240 game area; a 16:9 monitor uses
a centred 240×240 logical area, which becomes 4:3 when stretched to the panel.
If the monitor already pillarboxes 320×240 to 4:3, choose **4:3 monitor** here
to avoid applying the correction twice. This is a presentation choice, not a
claim that NES pixels are square or that every television had identical geometry.

The left/right and top/bottom masks independently cover 0, 8 or 16 original
NES pixels **per edge**. They do not change CPU/PPU emulation, expand the
remaining image, skip frames or stretch cropped content. The default hides
8 pixels at either side and leaves top/bottom visible. The selected border
fills the covered pixels and any widescreen side panels.

## Discussion and reference images requested by the owner

[SourMesen/Mesen-S issue #122](https://github.com/SourMesen/Mesen-S/issues/122)
discusses cropping and a CRT shader's edge effect. SourMesen explains that an
overscan setting removes pixels; it does not add extra picture information.
The comments also distinguish SNES vertical blank areas from the NES edge
garbage that horizontal masks can hide. Do not apply that thread's SNES
vertical-only recommendation indiscriminately to NES games.

These are **klepp0906's original comparison screenshots**, from that issue,
19 May 2020. They show SNES gameplay, an overlay and a CRT shader together;
they are reference images, not clean NES bezel textures or examples of this
